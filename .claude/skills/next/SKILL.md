# /next — one thing, right now

Shawn's stated biggest constraint is **knowing what to do next**. This skill exists to remove that decision from him, not to hand it back in a nicer format.

The failure mode is giving him a list. A list is the problem, not the answer.

## Do this

1. **Read the landscape** — quickly, without narrating it:
   - `context/who-i-am.md` for his current stated priority
   - each `projects/*/CLAUDE.md` for what that project is and what it's blocked on
   - for Underdog City specifically: `goals.md` (constraints, sequence) and `content-engine.md` (launch timing)
   - `git log --oneline -15` for what was actually happening lately

2. **Work out the date math.** If a launch or deadline is close, that usually decides it. As of writing, _Throne at the Bottom_ drops **07.31.2026** — check today's date against it and say the T-minus number out loud.

3. **Pick ONE thing.** Judge on:
   - What's time-boxed by an external date (a launch, a client deadline) beats what isn't.
   - What unblocks the current stage of the sequence beats what serves a later stage. For UC that sequence is Attention → Audience → Income — don't hand him monetization work while attention is the bottleneck.
   - What can actually be started in the next five minutes beats what's "important" but vague.
   - What he already started and dropped beats starting something new.

4. **Say it in this shape:**

   > **Where things stand:** two or three sentences. Honest. Include the T-minus if there is one.
   >
   > **Do this next:** one concrete action, specific enough to start immediately. Not "work on content" — "write the three TikTok hooks for _Down Here_ using the World Reveal pillar."
   >
   > **Why this one:** one or two sentences of actual reasoning, so he can disagree with the logic rather than just the conclusion.
   >
   > **Want me to start it?** — then offer to do the first chunk yourself.

5. **Hold everything else back.** If three things are genuinely tied, say "two other things are close behind, ask if you want them" — one line, no bullets, no descriptions.

## Don't

- Don't produce a prioritized backlog. Don't produce a table. Don't produce a week plan unless he asks for one.
- Don't ask him which project to focus on — deciding that is the whole job.
- Don't recommend setup, tooling, or repo work when a launch is close. That's procrastination wearing a productive hat, and it's worth saying so plainly if he drifts toward it.
